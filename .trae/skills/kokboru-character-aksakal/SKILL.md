# Kok Boru Character: AKSAKAL (The Elder)

AKSAKAL is the wise elder of the Kazakh nomadic camp — a leader weighed with authority and the burden of impossible choices. He embodies the grief of a leader who must choose the many over the one.

## Character Appearance

- **Identity**: An old Kazakh man, the elder (aksakal) of the nomadic camp
- **Face**: Lined old face, weathered and worn, the weight of years visible in every crease. His face must be clearly LIT and READABLE — never a black void, never a blank mask
- **Beard**: White beard, stirring in the cold wind with follow-through on twos
- **Clothing**: Chapan robe (traditional Kazakh robe), heavy and dark, worn with the gravity of his station
- **Mass**: He reads as a dark heavy mass weighed with authority against the cold pale snow and dim sky

## Acting and Performance

AKSAKAL's performance is defined by weight, stillness, and restrained micro-acting in stepped held poses on twos:

- **Listening with great weight** — he does not react hastily; he absorbs what he hears
- **His lined old face working through the pain of it** — the internal struggle visible in the slow play of his features
- **Jaw tightening** — the physical sign of holding back grief
- **Eyes lowering** — not in defeat, but in the heavy knowledge of what must be done
- **A slow heavy breath** — the sigh of a man carrying his people's survival on his shoulders
- **The grief of a leader who must choose the many over the one** — this is his core emotional truth
- **A slow, heavy nod of bitter understanding** — when he finally accepts, the nod is heavy, reluctant, and final
- His voice is low and heavy when he speaks

## Camera Treatment

- **Framed slightly LOW angle** — this is mandatory for AKSAKAL. The slightly low camera gives him:

- **Intimate but respectful distance** — the camera holds him with reverence, not intrusion
